# Short write-up (≈285 words) — paste into the submission form

**Problem.** The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) manages the Texas Interconnection, which supplies power to about 90% of the state’s electric load. Most residents share that one independent grid; other parts of Texas use completely different systems. ERCOT publishes load, wind, solar, settlement prices, and congestion, but most tools stop at raw charts. Analysts still cannot quickly see which hours are *jointly* unusual, which measurements support that claim, or what a constrained home-battery fleet would have done on the same clock — especially when zones like Houston diverge from the hub.

**Who it helps.** Base Power (Austin) and anyone building on open Texas grid data who needs a reproducible, evidence-first read of ERCOT public feeds — without pretending to dispatch hardware or move nodal prices. That includes teams tracking how statewide demand and renewables show up as stress and locational price gaps for the majority of Texans on ERCOT.

**Solution.** GridSignal pulls the ERCOT Public API (or runs offline from a saved week / synthetic demo), normalizes series to UTC, flags anomalies with past-only baselines, scores a transparent GridSignal Stress Indicator that never treats missing as zero, attaches Facts / Interpretation / Hypothesis plus evidence IDs, and simulates idle, causal, hybrid, and labeled-oracle battery strategies under real SOC and power limits. Live demo: https://basegridsignaltabreadme-ov-file-mobbixbp7vrkxx7jckakjd.streamlit.app/

**Impact on Austin and Texas.** For the ~90% of Texas load on ERCOT — including Austin — GridSignal surfaces hub–zone spreads, load-up/wind-down co-moves, and the ledger gap between idle and a causal hybrid home-battery policy on a real saved week. Faster, honest answers about when the interconnection was tight and what residential storage *could* have done. Limits stay clear: stress is not an ERCOT rating, dollars are MWh×price not market impact, and ERCOT does not endorse this. Clone the repo, copy `.env.example`, and reproduce tomorrow.
